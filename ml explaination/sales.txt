1. First understand the practical
1.1 What is the aim of this practical?
The aim is to perform customer segmentation using K-Means and Hierarchical Clustering algorithms on a sales dataset.

In simple words, we want to divide customers into different groups based on their purchasing behaviour.

For example:

Imagine you own an online shopping company. You have 100 customers.

Some customers purchase products frequently.

Some customers spend a lot of money.

Some customers purchase only occasionally.

Some customers purchase large quantities but spend less money.

Would you treat all these customers in the same way?

Probably not!

You might want to:

Give discounts to frequent customers.

Offer premium membership to high-spending customers.

Send promotional offers to occasional customers.

Encourage low-spending customers to purchase more.

But how will you identify these different types of customers?

This is where Customer Segmentation comes into the picture.

1.2 What is Customer Segmentation?
Customer segmentation means dividing customers into different groups based on similarities in their characteristics or behaviour.

For example:

1.3 What is Clustering?
Clustering is an Unsupervised Machine Learning technique.

Let's understand this term.

Machine Learning is broadly divided into three categories:

Type

Meaning

Supervised Learning

Learns from labelled data.

Unsupervised Learning

Finds patterns in data without predefined labels.

Reinforcement Learning

Learns through rewards and penalties.

What is Unsupervised Learning?
Suppose you give a computer information about 100 customers.

You don't tell it which customer is:

Rich

Regular

Occasional

Low-spending

You only provide their sales information.

The computer analyses the similarities and automatically forms groups.

This is called Unsupervised Learning.

Important: Clustering does not require predefined output labels. It discovers groups from the input data.

1.4 What are the two algorithms used?
A. K-Means Clustering
K-Means Clustering : What I Learned In Unsupervised Learning | by Aniket | Feb, 2026 | Medium

K-Means divides data points into a predefined number of clusters, represented by K.

For example, if K = 3, the algorithm creates three clusters.

It works by:

Selecting K initial centroids.

Assigning each data point to its nearest centroid.

Calculating new centroids.

Repeating the process until the assignments stop changing significantly.

What is a centroid?

A centroid is the centre or average position of all the data points belonging to a cluster.

Example:

Suppose we have three customers with spending values:

B. Hierarchical Clustering
Mastering Clustering Algorithms for Effective Data Analysis

Hierarchical Clustering creates a hierarchy of clusters.

It has two approaches:

Approach

Meaning

Agglomerative

Bottom-up approach: starts with individual points and merges them.

Divisive

Top-down approach: starts with one large cluster and splits it.

Your practical uses Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering.

It starts by treating every customer as an individual cluster and gradually combines similar clusters.

What is the Elbow Method?
The Elbow Method is a technique used to estimate a suitable number of clusters in K-Means.

It calculates the inertia for different values of K and plots them on a graph.

We look for a point where the reduction in inertia starts slowing down considerably. This point resembles an elbow.

First understand inertia
Inertia measures the sum of squared distances between each data point and the centroid of its assigned cluster.

In simple language:

It measures how closely the data points are grouped around their respective cluster centres.

Lower inertia generally means tighter clusters.

However, increasing K usually reduces inertia, so the lowest inertia alone does not tell us the best K.

How close the data point is to other points in its own cluster.

13.2 How exactly does Agglomerative Clustering work?
The Beginner’s Guide to Clustering in Machine Learning | by Utsav Desai | Medium

It follows these steps:

Initially, every customer is treated as an individual cluster.

Calculate the distances between clusters.

Find the closest clusters.

Merge those clusters.

Recalculate distances using the selected linkage method.

Repeat until the required number of clusters remains.

In your practical:

Initial clusters = 92.

Final clusters = 4.

Therefore, 88 merging operations are performed.

Difference between linkage and distance
These two concepts are related but different.

Distance measures how far two individual points are from one another.

Linkage determines how the distance between two groups of points is calculated.

For example, single linkage uses the minimum pairwise distance, while Ward linkage uses the increase in within-cluster variance.

Your code uses Ward linkage for the dendrogram. Your AgglomerativeClustering call does not explicitly specify linkage, so it uses Scikit-learn's default linkage="ward".
